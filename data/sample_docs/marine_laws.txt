INTERNATIONAL AND NATIONAL MARINE LAWS AND REGULATIONS
=======================================================

1. UNITED NATIONS CONVENTION ON THE LAW OF THE SEA (UNCLOS)
-------------------------------------------------------------
UNCLOS (1982) is the primary international framework governing the use of
the world's oceans. It establishes the rights and responsibilities of
nations with respect to their use of the world's oceans.

Key jurisdictional zones under UNCLOS:
- Territorial Sea (0–12 nautical miles): coastal state exercises full
  sovereignty; foreign vessels have right of innocent passage
- Contiguous Zone (12–24 nautical miles): coastal state may enforce laws
  relating to customs, taxation, immigration, and pollution
- Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ, 12–200 nautical miles): coastal state has
  sovereign rights for exploring, exploiting, conserving, and managing
  natural resources; other states have freedom of navigation and overflight
- Continental Shelf: coastal state has rights over the seabed and subsoil
  beyond the EEZ up to 350 nautical miles in some circumstances
- High Seas (beyond 200 nautical miles): open to all states; governed by
  flag state jurisdiction

Article 61 – Conservation of living resources (EEZ):
The coastal State shall determine the allowable catch of the living
resources in its EEZ. The coastal State, taking into account the best
scientific evidence available, shall ensure through proper conservation and
management measures that the maintenance of the living resources in the EEZ
is not endangered by over-exploitation.

Article 119 – Conservation of living resources of the high seas:
States shall take measures based on scientific data to maintain or restore
target species populations at levels which can produce maximum sustainable
yield (MSY).

2. CONVENTION ON BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY (CBD)
--------------------------------------------
The CBD (1992) has three main objectives:
- Conservation of biological diversity
- Sustainable use of its components
- Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources

Kunming-Montreal Framework (2022) – Key marine targets:
- Target 3 (30×30): Protect at least 30% of global ocean area by 2030
- Target 2: Restore 30% of degraded marine and coastal ecosystems
- Target 7: Reduce pollution, including excess nutrients and pesticides,
  to levels not harmful to biodiversity

3. INTERNATIONAL WHALING COMMISSION (IWC) REGULATIONS
------------------------------------------------------
The International Convention for the Regulation of Whaling (1946) established
the IWC with powers to regulate all aspects of whaling.

Current status:
- Commercial whaling moratorium in effect since 1986 (Schedule paragraph 10(e))
- Aboriginal subsistence whaling allowed for specific indigenous communities
- Scientific whaling: limited permits issued under Article VIII of the convention
- Cetacean sanctuaries: Southern Ocean Sanctuary (1994) and Indian Ocean
  Sanctuary (1979) prohibit all commercial whaling within their boundaries

4. REGIONAL FISHERIES MANAGEMENT ORGANIZATIONS (RFMOs)
-------------------------------------------------------
RFMOs manage fish stocks in international waters:

ICES (International Council for the Exploration of the Sea):
- Provides scientific advice for the North Atlantic and adjacent seas
- Stock assessments conducted annually; precautionary approach reference
  points (Blim, Bpa, Flim, Fpa) defined for each managed stock

ICCAT (International Commission for the Conservation of Atlantic Tunas):
- Manages Atlantic tunas and tuna-like species
- Current Bluefin tuna quotas: Eastern stock TAC = 36,000 tonnes/year;
  Western stock TAC = 2,350 tonnes/year (effective 2024)
- Minimum size limits: Atlantic Bluefin Tuna 115 cm curved fork length

NAFO (Northwest Atlantic Fisheries Organization):
- Manages fisheries resources in the Northwest Atlantic outside 200 nm EEZs
- Mesh size requirements: minimum 130 mm for groundfish trawls
- Observer coverage: 100% observer coverage required in NAFO Regulatory Area

5. EUROPEAN UNION COMMON FISHERIES POLICY (CFP)
------------------------------------------------
The EU CFP provides the framework for managing EU fishing fleets and
conserving fish stocks.

Key principles:
- Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY): fishing at or below MSY levels for all stocks
- Multi-Annual Plans (MAPs): multi-year management strategies for stock groups
- Technical measures regulation (EU 2019/1241): gear specifications, area
  closures, minimum conservation reference sizes (MCRS)
- Landing obligation (discard ban): since 2019, all catches of regulated
  species must be landed (with limited exceptions for high survival rates)

Minimum Conservation Reference Sizes (MCRS) – Selected species:
- Atlantic Cod (Gadus morhua): 35 cm total length
- Atlantic Haddock (Melanogrammus aeglefinus): 30 cm total length
- European Hake (Merluccius merluccius): 27 cm total length
- Atlantic Herring (Clupea harengus): 18 cm total length
- Nephrops (Nephrops norvegicus): 25 mm carapace length

6. MARINE POLLUTION PREVENTION REGULATIONS
-------------------------------------------
MARPOL 73/78 (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships):

Annex I – Oil: prohibited discharge zones within 50 nautical miles of shore;
maximum discharge rate 30 litres per nautical mile
Annex II – Noxious liquid substances: tiered discharge requirements by category
Annex IV – Sewage: no discharge within 3 nautical miles of nearest land
Annex V – Garbage: plastic discharge prohibited in all sea areas
Annex VI – Air pollution: 0.5% global sulphur cap on marine fuel (since 2020);
0.1% in Emission Control Areas (ECAs) including Baltic Sea, North Sea,
North American coast

7. NATIONAL FISHERIES MANAGEMENT REGULATIONS
---------------------------------------------
Total Allowable Catches (TACs) and quotas are set annually based on
scientific stock assessments and international negotiations.

Licensing and access:
- Fishing vessels must hold a valid fishing licence issued by flag state authority
- Vessel Monitoring Systems (VMS): mandatory for vessels > 15 m overall length
- Electronic logbooks: mandatory recording of all catch, effort, and
  transshipment data for vessels > 10 m overall length

Prohibited practices:
- Dynamite or explosive fishing: prohibited under all circumstances
- Poison/toxicant use: strictly prohibited
- Bottom trawling: prohibited in designated deep-sea sensitive areas and
  within 4 nautical miles of coast where depth < 100 m

Seasonal restrictions:
- Spawning closures: specific areas and periods designated annually
- Shrimp fishery seasonal window: open June 1 – November 30 only
- Lobster closed season: May 1 – August 31 in northern zones
